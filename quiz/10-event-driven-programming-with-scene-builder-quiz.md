## Answer Key

> Read this section only after completing all questions.

---

**Q1 - B**  
*Correct because:* **JavaFX** is used as a chapter concept that shapes design, implementation, or verification, not as decoration.  
*Why the distractors are wrong:*  
- A) This reflects the misconception that chapter vocabulary is cosmetic rather than operational.  
- C) This reflects the misconception that naming a concept removes the need for reasoning.  
*Chapter reference:* What MVC Actually Is

---

**Q2 - A**  
*Correct because:* The chapter ties **scene graph** to project work so students can connect Java mechanisms to responsibilities, state, behavior, and evidence.  
*Why the distractors are wrong:*  
- B) This treats the idea as a narrow syntax exercise.  
- C) This confuses explanation support with replacement of student judgment.  
*Chapter reference:* The Scene Graph

---

**Q3 - False**  
*Correct because:* The chapter's recurring habit is to verify early and repeatedly through traces, tests, setup evidence, or focused checks. Waiting until the end makes causes harder to isolate.  
*Chapter reference:* TableView and the Question It Forces

---

**Q4 - C**  
*Correct because:* **pane** is presented as something students use to make choices and explain consequences in a working application.  
*Why the distractors are wrong:*  
- A) This separates the concept from the project reasoning the chapter emphasizes.  
- B) This mistakes a chapter label for a usable engineering idea.  
*Chapter reference:* The Search Bar Problem

---

**Q5 - True**  
*Correct because:* The chapter treats AI as assistance within boundaries: the student must still understand the code, verify behavior, and own the explanation.  
*Chapter reference:* The Event Handler and Where It Belongs

---

**Q6 - C**  
*Correct because:* The scenario lacks ownership and evidence. The correct next move is to trace responsibility and verify behavior in a focused way.  
*Why the distractors are wrong:*  
- A) A single successful run is weak evidence when the responsibility is unclear.  
- B) AI output still needs verification and a student-owned explanation.  
*Chapter reference:* The Panes and What They Are For

---

**Q7 - Short-Answer**  
*Model answer:* **TableView** helps by giving the student a specific concept to trace through objects, state, behavior, or evidence rather than treating the program as disconnected lines of code.  
*Common error:* A weak answer merely defines the term without explaining how it supports system understanding.  
*Chapter reference:* Keeping Display Out of the Model

---

**Q8 - A**  
*Correct because:* The chapter repeatedly pushes students from copying a visible pattern toward understanding the mechanism that will still hold when conditions change.  
*Why the distractors are wrong:*  
- B) This misses the connection between procedures and Java artifacts.  
- C) This wrongly confines design thinking to reflection instead of implementation.  
*Chapter reference:* Where AI Fits Into This Picture

---
**Q9 - Brief-Response**  
*Model answer:* A strong defense would name the expected behavior for **view**, show the object or component responsible for it, and provide evidence such as a trace, test, focused run, or setup packet. It would also explain what failure would look like and how the chosen evidence rules out that failure.  
*What a strong answer includes:*  
- A specific chapter concept connected to a concrete project responsibility.  
- A justified form of evidence rather than a general claim that the feature works.  
*Common error:* Describing the final output without explaining the mechanism or verification path.  
*Chapter reference:* The Module's Single Capability

---

**Q10 - Transfer Brief-Response**  
*Model answer:* In a clinic scheduling or inventory system, **controller** would still require clear ownership of state, explicit behavior, and evidence that the behavior survives realistic changes. The domain vocabulary and entities would change, but the need to connect model decisions, user actions, and verification would remain.  
*What a strong answer includes:*  
- The chapter principle applied correctly to a new context.  
- A distinction between surface domain details and the shared design structure.  
*Chapter reference:* Core chapter principle: controller
